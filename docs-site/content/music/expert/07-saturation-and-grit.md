## Why distortion sounds "full"

When a wave is pushed too far, it gets clipped. The clipped edges add extra frequencies (harmonics), so the sound gets thicker and louder-sounding.

## Bitcrusher

A **bitcrusher** reduces how finely the sound is measured, so it sounds like an old video game.

## Where it fits

Light saturation on drums and bass glues them together. Heavy distortion is for rock, noise and special effects.

{{deeper Even and odd harmonics}}
Different kinds of distortion add different harmonics. **Tube-style** saturation adds mostly even harmonics (2nd, 4th), which sound warm and musical. **Hard clipping** adds many odd harmonics, which sound harsh and aggressive. **Tape** saturation also gently reduces the highs and softens peaks. Choosing the type is choosing the flavour.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Gain before distortion}}
How much a distortion changes a sound depends on how loud the signal is when it arrives. A quiet signal passes almost untouched; a loud one is crushed. Many producers therefore lower the input to keep the sound clean and raise it for the dirty version. That makes level the main control of a distortion.
{{/deeper}}
