## The family

All of these use a slow wave (an LFO) to change something a little.

| Effect | What moves | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Tremolo | volume | pulsing |
| Vibrato | pitch | wavering |
| Chorus | a copy delayed slightly | thick, wide |
| Flanger | a very short delay with feedback | jet-like sweep |
| Phaser | phase of the sound | swirling |

## When to use them

A pad with a little chorus feels wide and warm. A flanger on a drum fill adds drama. As with everything: a small amount does the job.

{{deeper What an LFO controls}}
An LFO (low-frequency oscillator) is too slow to hear as a note (below about 20 Hz). It acts as a hand that turns a knob over and over. It can control:

- **Pitch**: vibrato.
- **Volume**: tremolo.
- **Filter cutoff**: a wobble or "wah".
- **Pan**: a sound that moves from side to side.

Rate sets the speed, depth sets how much, and shape sets the pattern (a smooth sine, a ramp, a step). Syncing the rate to the tempo makes the movement part of the groove.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Why chorus sounds thick}}
A chorus mixes the original with a slightly delayed and slightly out-of-tune copy. Two nearly identical sounds with small timing and pitch differences sound like several players at once. The slow movement of the delay time keeps the difference changing, so the ear cannot lock on it.
{{/deeper}}
