## Inversions

A chord does not need to start on its root. Move the lowest note to the top and you get an **inversion**. It is still C major, but it sounds a bit different.

## Open and close

Close voicing packs the notes within one octave. Open voicing spreads them out and sounds wider.

{{piano {"from":48,"to":76,"hi":[48,64,67,72],"play":"chord","caption":"An open C chord: a low C, then E, G and C above."}}}

{{deeper Doubling and spacing}}
In a four-voice chord you often **double** a note (play it twice, in different octaves). Doubling the root is safest; doubling the third makes a major chord sound sharp. Big gaps between the lower voices muddy the sound: keep the notes in the bass range at least an octave apart, and the notes above close together.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Guitar and piano voicings}}
A piano usually plays the root in the left hand and the other notes in the right. A guitar is limited to six strings, so it uses chord shapes with doubled notes. Because of these differences, the same chord sounds "wide and open" on guitar and "tight" on piano. Choose a voicing that suits the instrument.
{{/deeper}}
