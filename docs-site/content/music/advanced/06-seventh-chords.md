## The seventh

A **seventh chord** is a triad plus the note a seventh above the root. There are three common kinds.

| Chord | Example | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| major seventh | Cmaj7 (C E G B) | dreamy, soft, "cafe" |
| minor seventh | Am7 (A C E G) | warm, mellow |
| dominant seventh | G7 (G B D F) | tense, wants to resolve |

## Tension and release

The dominant seventh (G7) contains a tense interval (B against F). Your ear wants it to go to C. When it does, that is **resolution**, the satisfying landing at the end of a phrase.

## Sus, add and more

- **sus4** replaces the third with a fourth: open and unresolved (Csus4).
- **add9** adds a ninth on top: shimmering (Cadd9).
- **6** adds a sixth: old-fashioned and sweet.

{{deeper Why jazz uses so many of these}}
Triads are clear but plain. Adding sevenths, ninths and thirteenths creates more notes to choose from and more tension to resolve, which is the material of improvisation. The idea is the same as before, just with more notes stacked on the chord.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper The tritone}}
The interval of six semitones (half an octave) is the **tritone**. It is the most unstable interval, and sits inside every dominant seventh chord: in G7, the notes B and F are a tritone apart. Because the pull to resolve is so strong, the tritone in V7 is what makes the move to I feel like arrival.

Jazz players use it for **tritone substitution**: you can replace the dominant chord with the one a tritone away (for G7, use Db7) because they share the same tense notes.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Extended chords: 9, 11, 13}}
Keep stacking thirds above the seventh and you get the extended chords. A ninth is the second note of the scale an octave up, an eleventh is the fourth, a thirteenth is the sixth.

| Chord | Notes (from C) | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Cmaj9 | C E G B D | lush, floating |
| C9 | C E G Bb D | funky, bluesy |
| Cm9 | C Eb G Bb D | smooth, soulful |
| C13 | C E G Bb D A | big, jazzy |

In practice you do not play all notes. Leave out the fifth, and sometimes the root if the bass plays it.
{{/deeper}}
