## The parts

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Intro | sets the mood |
| Verse | tells the story, changes each time |
| Pre-chorus | builds tension |
| Chorus | the main idea, repeats, is the loudest |
| Bridge | a change, something new |
| Outro | ends the song |

A common order is **intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus, outro**.

## Length

Parts come in groups of 4, 8 or 16 bars so that changes land on a downbeat. A verse is often 8 or 16 bars.

## Contrast

A chorus feels big because the verse before it was smaller. Change something between parts: add a drum, remove the bass, raise the key, change the volume.

{{deeper Common song forms}}
| Form | Plan | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Verse-chorus | A (verse), B (chorus), repeat | most pop |
| AABA (32-bar) | A, A, bridge, A | jazz standards |
| 12-bar blues | three four-bar lines, same chords every time | blues, early rock |
| Strophic | the same music for every verse | folk songs, hymns |
| Through-composed | no repeats | classical, some film music |
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Moving between parts}}
A change of section is announced: a **fill** (drums), a **riser** (a rising noise), a **breakdown** (everything drops away for a moment) or a **pickup** (a few notes before the downbeat). These are not decorations; they tell the listener that something is about to change.
{{/deeper}}
