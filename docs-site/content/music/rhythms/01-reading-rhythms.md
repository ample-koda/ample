## One bar, sixteen steps

A bar in 4/4 has four beats. Split every beat in four and you get **sixteen steps** (sixteenth notes), counted "1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a". In the patterns on these pages `x` is a hit and `.` is silence. This pattern hits only on the four beats:

`x . . . x . . . x . . . x . . .`

## What you hear in the previews

A bright high tick plays the rhythm. A low soft tick is the steady beat (1 2 3 4). Keep the beat in your head, or tap it with your foot, and listen where the high ticks land against it: on the beat, between beats, or just after.

{{deeper Why do the patterns repeat?}}
Most rhythms here are **cycles**: a short pattern that goes round and round. A cycle is usually one bar, but a waltz has a cycle of three beats and a 5/4 pattern five. Repeating is what lets your body predict the next hit, and that prediction is what makes a rhythm feel like a groove.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Steps, pulses and beats}}
A **beat** is what you tap your foot to. A **step** or **pulse** is a smaller, equal slice of it. Sixteen steps per bar is the usual grid for straight rhythms. Rhythms that feel rolling, such as the shuffle, use a grid of twelve pulses per bar (three per beat). Each page says which grid it uses.
{{/deeper}}
