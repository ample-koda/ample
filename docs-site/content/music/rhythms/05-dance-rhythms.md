## Waltz

Three beats to the bar, felt as **ONE two three**: a low "oom" on the first beat and two lighter "pah"s after it. The weight on beat one makes dancers turn.

`oom pah pah`

{{deeper Origin}}
The waltz comes from Austria and southern Germany, from the country dance called the Ländler, and became the rage in Vienna at the end of the 18th century. The word comes from the German *walzen*, "to roll" or "to turn". Johann Strauss II made it world famous with "The Blue Danube" (1866), and Chopin turned it into concert piano music.
{{/deeper}}

## Bossa nova

The bossa nova guitar pattern is a **clave-like** pattern of five hits, gentle and a little late, over a soft beat. It sways instead of marching.

`x . . x . . x . . . x . . x . .`

{{deeper Origin}}
Bossa nova started in Rio de Janeiro in the late 1950s. The name means roughly "new trend". The guitarist and singer João Gilberto, the composer Antônio Carlos Jobim and the poet Vinicius de Moraes made it, and "Chega de Saudade" (around 1958) is usually named as its first great record. "The Girl from Ipanema" followed in the early 1960s.
{{/deeper}}
