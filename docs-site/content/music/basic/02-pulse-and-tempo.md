## Beat

The **beat** (or pulse) is the steady tick underneath a song. Clap along to a song you know and you are clapping the beat.

## Tempo

**Tempo** is how many beats there are per minute, written **bpm**.

| Tempo | Feel |
|---|---|
| 60 bpm | one beat per second, calm |
| 90 bpm | relaxed walk |
| 120 bpm | brisk walk, a lot of pop and dance |
| 160+ bpm | very fast |

## Bars

Beats are grouped. The first beat of a group is stronger (you hear the higher tick in the preview). One group is a **bar** (or measure). Most songs use four beats per bar: **1** 2 3 4, **1** 2 3 4.

{{deeper Does a faster tempo change the pitch?}}
No. Tempo changes how fast notes come, not how high they are. Pitch and tempo are separate: you can play the same melody slowly or quickly.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Tempo words from classical music}}
Before metronomes, composers described speed in Italian. They are still used:

| Word | Roughly (bpm) | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Largo | 40-60 | very slow, broad |
| Adagio | 66-76 | slow, calm |
| Andante | 76-108 | walking pace |
| Moderato | 108-120 | moderate |
| Allegro | 120-156 | fast, lively |
| Presto | 168-200 | very fast |

Tempo can also change in a song: **accelerando** (speeding up), **ritardando** (slowing down), and **fermata** (hold a note as long as you like).
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Beat versus rhythm}}
The **beat** is the steady pulse. The **rhythm** is the pattern of notes that is played over it. A melody can have a complicated rhythm and still sit on a plain beat. If you tap your foot steadily and clap the rhythm of a song you know, you are doing both at once, which is the basis of all playing.
{{/deeper}}
