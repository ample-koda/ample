## Shape

Look at where a melody goes: up, down, or around. A melody that climbs feels like a question. One that falls feels like an answer. Mostly melodies move by **steps** (neighbouring notes) and now and then make a **leap**.

## Motif

A **motif** is a short idea (3 to 5 notes). A good melody repeats the motif and changes it a little: higher, lower, with a different ending. Repetition is what makes you remember a tune.

## Rest points

A melody needs places to rest, usually on notes that sound stable (1, 3 and 5 of the scale). Ending on 1 sounds finished. Ending on another note feels like "to be continued".

{{deeper Range and contour}}
**Range** is the distance between the lowest and the highest note. A melody that stays within an octave and a half is easy to sing and remember. **Contour** is the shape: an arch (up then down), a wave, a staircase. Most memorable tunes have a clear contour with one high point, the climax.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Phrases and sentences}}
A melody is built like a sentence. A **phrase** is a musical thought, often 2 or 4 bars long. Two phrases make a **sentence**: the first asks (ends away from home), the second answers (ends on home). When you write a melody, think in questions and answers rather than in single notes.
{{/deeper}}
