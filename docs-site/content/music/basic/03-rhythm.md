## The three voices

- **Kick**: the low thump. It marks the beat.
- **Snare**: the sharp crack. It marks the **backbeat**, beats 2 and 4.
- **Hi-hat**: the light tick. It fills the space in between.

## Read it like a grid

Each eight-step row is one bar. `x` is a hit, `.` is silence. The kick falls on 1 and 3, the snare on 2 and 4.

```
hat:    x x x x x x x x
snare:  . . x . . . x .
kick:   x . . . x . . .
```

## Change one thing

Move one hit and the groove changes. Here the kick plays on every beat ("four on the floor", the basis of dance music):

{{deeper Swing}}
Straight eighth notes are evenly spaced. **Swing** makes every second eighth late, like a skipping step. It is why jazz feels relaxed and bouncy. The same rhythm, played with swing, feels different.
{{/deeper}}

{{try Build your own}}
Take the first beat and change one position of the kick. Does it still feel like it moves forward? Dance music keeps the kick steady and moves the other parts. Rock moves the kick around.
{{/try}}

{{deeper Fills and ghost notes}}
A **fill** is a short, busy pattern at the end of a section that signals "something new starts". It breaks the groove on purpose, so the new section feels like an arrival. **Ghost notes** are very quiet extra hits (usually on the snare) between the main beats. They make a groove feel alive without making it busy.
{{/deeper}}
