## The four places

| Place | Tool | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Level | volume | what should be loudest? |
| Left and right | pan | where does it sit? |
| Frequency | EQ and filter | what does it share with others? |
| Depth | reverb and delay | close or far? |

## A routine

1. **Start with the rhythm**: kick and bass, at a healthy level.
2. **Add harmony** and make it fit.
3. **Place the melody**: it should be clear but not shouting.
4. **Add effects** (reverb, delay) in small amounts.
5. **Check at low volume**: if it still works, the balance is good.

{{deeper Gain staging}}
Keep each channel's level in a healthy range and leave room at the top of the master. If everything is near the top, any boost makes the sound clip. Lower the faders, not the master.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Balancing by ear: a method}}
1. Set all faders low, bring up the **kick and bass** first until they feel solid.
2. Add the **drums** until they sit with the bass.
3. Add the **main harmony** and the **main melody**, listening to the whole.
4. Add the **supporting parts** very quietly. If you cannot hear them, they are fine; if you notice them missing when you mute them, they have a job.
5. Check that the **loudest part** of the song is the part you want to be loudest.

Listen on different speakers and at a low volume. A mix that holds together quietly is balanced.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Common mixing problems and fixes}}
| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Muddy | too much energy at 200-400 Hz | cut a little on several parts, high-pass non-bass |
| Harsh | too much at 2-5 kHz | cut, or use a de-esser/limited saturation |
| No punch | compressor too fast, or kick and bass fight | slower attack, separate their ranges |
| Flat | everything dry and centred | add depth with reverb and pan |
| Thin | missing low-mids | gentle boost, check phase |
{{/deeper}}
