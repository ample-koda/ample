## Make small songs

Finish short pieces. A finished 30-second piece teaches more than an unfinished ten-minute epic.

## Reference

Compare your song with a song you love. Same loudness, same section. What is different? Bass? Brightness? Space?

## Take breaks

After an hour your ears tire. Leave the song, come back later, and listen with fresh ears.

## Save versions

Save under new names as you go, so you can go back.

{{deeper A simple weekly routine}}
- **Day 1-2**: write a loop (drums, bass, chords) and a melody.
- **Day 3**: arrange it into sections.
- **Day 4**: sound design and effects.
- **Day 5**: mix.
- **Day 6**: rest. Do not touch it.
- **Day 7**: listen again, fix small things, finish. Start the next song.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Overcoming getting stuck}}
- Remove something instead of adding. A part you do not need is often what holds you.
- Change the key, the tempo or the instrument for a moment.
- Constrain yourself: only four notes, only one chord, only a drum and a bass.
- Finish a worse version quickly, and learn from it.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper Layering: two almost-equal sounds make one fuller sound}}
Split a sound, process each part differently (one dark, one bright, one a little detuned or delayed) and mix them back. Each part keeps its own place in level, pan and frequency, and together they sound wider and fuller than one copy.
{{/deeper}}
