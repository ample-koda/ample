## What mastering is

Mixing balances the parts. Mastering treats the finished mix: a light EQ, gentle compression, a limiter, and a check that it works on all speakers.

## A simple chain

1. **EQ**: small moves only (1 to 2 dB).
2. **Compression**: gently glue it together.
3. **Limiter**: set the final level.
4. **Check**: on headphones, a phone, a car.

## A song as an album

When several songs belong together, they should sit at a similar level and tone. That is the job of the mastering stage.

{{deeper Checking your master}}
- **Mono check**: sum to mono. If parts disappear or sound hollow, there are phase problems.
- **Small speaker check**: a phone or a laptop. Does the bass line still come through?
- **Reference check**: play your song next to a released one at the same loudness. Compare brightness and low end.
- **Quiet and loud check**: at low volume you should hear the main parts; at high volume it should not hurt.
{{/deeper}}

{{deeper What to leave alone}}
Mastering is not a fix for a bad mix. Large problems (a part too loud, a muddy low end) are better solved in the mix. A mastering EQ should rarely move more than 2 dB. If you feel you need more, go back to the mix.
{{/deeper}}
